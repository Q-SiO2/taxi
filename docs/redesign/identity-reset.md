## Goal and non-negotiable creative bar

Establish a recognizable TaxiMobile art system through vehicle silhouette,
material, composition, typography, color relationships and authored movement.
The ambition is exceptional distinctiveness. Do not promise that nothing like it
exists anywhere: a global originality claim cannot be verified by this exercise.

1. **Identity survives recoloring.** In grayscale, and with the same appearance
   theme applied, the directions must remain distinguishable by form, composition,
   material and movement. Changing the primary hue is not a new art direction.
2. **Color is a relationship.** Use several purposeful complementary colors in
   artwork and selected interface elements. Avoid monochrome washes across the
   entire screen. Functional text/status contrast remains an acceptance condition.
3. **Artwork must earn its place.** A main vehicle/city illustration is not required.
   Build the signature through type, framing, material and motion. If vehicle art
   is reconsidered later, develop a consistent authored silhouette rather than
   a stock sedan surrounded by colored blobs.
4. **Space is coherent.** Tire contact, road plane, perspective, shadow, occlusion
   and movement direction must make sense. A decorative line cannot stand in for
   a road or server route and pass arbitrarily behind the vehicle.
5. **Animation has a performance.** Use staging, exposure cadence, material response,
   depth and a memorable rest pose. Merely translating a flat picture is not a
   finished vehicle animation. Brand motion must not imply live supply or assignment.
6. **The interface inherits the world.** Shape, edges, typography, selected states,
   receipt/offer structure and page transitions must carry the same grammar.
   Artwork above otherwise identical generic cards does not establish the system.
7. **The complete app remains the scope.** Retain the 213-view structural inventory.
   Once the direction is selected, revisit each view and all 40 component contracts
   under that art system; do not substitute a few hero screens for full coverage.

## Returning to the vehicle reference

[BUCK's Rivian project](https://buck.co/work/rivian) describes translating
hand-drawn 2D styling into toon-shaded spatial work, and using sprite layers and
parallax around a vehicle model. The page was reopened and its vehicle/display
imagery visually inspected. This is craft evidence; no claim is made to have
watched every film or validated its implementation performance.

The previous proposal flattened this reference into a small vector scene and
prematurely limited spatial exploration. The useful transfer is an authored
vehicle and a consistent drawn world across dimensions. Copying Rivian's car,
dashboard, nature imagery or engine architecture is unnecessary.

[BUCK's Cash App illustration study](https://buck.co/work/cash-app-illustration-system)
also makes illustration tone and in-situ role part of the system. Here that means
judging the art inside fare/offer/receipt pages, rather than assuming a strong
poster makes a strong application. The new interpretation is TaxiMobile's own
proposal, not a finding measured on TaxiMobile users.

## Motion and production

The current lab animates four framing groups around a stable fare-review
composition. Glazed framing uses held settling poses; Street press uses compact
registration accents; Folded panels use sequenced small hinges. Text, fare facts
and action geometry remain stationary. It lasts 2,400 ms, runs only on replay and
returns to a static rest pose. Stop, scrub, reduced motion and hidden/offscreen
cancellation are provided. This is interface motion, not vehicle motion.

The preceding vehicle staging investigation remains historical concept work.
Its separate transparent prop and road/contact-shadow exploration demonstrated
a route out of the incoherent old city vector, but the owner still does not find
the main illustrations suitable. Do not force them into the current interface.
No claim is made that a raster prop is a rotating 3D model or that its wheels animate.

Production candidates to prove after the graphic grammar is selected:


- **2D cel art:** layered original drawings and a deliberately bounded exposure
  sequence, static key pose, redraws for wheels/vehicle deformation where needed.
- **2.5D art:** transparent illustrated layers and cached drawing paths; separate
  tire/shadow/body/background coordinates and explicit overlap rules. This is the
  first candidate for a scoped native prototype, not a rule that precludes 3D art.
- **3D-authored, rendered assets:** commission/build a reusable taxi model and
  materials, render approved angles/short performances, and measure actual asset
  size, decoded memory, transparency and delivery behavior before choosing format.
- **Runtime 3D:** a separate bounded feasibility study only if interaction requires
  actual changing viewpoints. No new engine or KMP runtime is adopted by this doc.

[Compose's official drawing overview](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/graphics/draw/overview)
supports custom drawing and transforms as a delivery mechanism for the simpler
layered case. Native clipping/layout/density and caching must be checked in the
actual checkout. The existing bounded PNG artwork loader remains the source
constraint; a browser SVG/master cannot simply be fed to that PNG decoder.

Numbers to establish through a prototype, rather than invent as accepted budgets:
decode memory, launch/decode latency, frame time on the connected Android device,
package increment, lifecycle cleanup and browser/iOS fallback. No performance,
device, alpha or launch acceptance follows from these browser specimens.
